About

I use spatial data to find patterns that point to illegal activity at sea and on land, with a focus on illegal fishing, labor and human trafficking, and conservation. I'm based in Ames, Iowa, and finishing my Master of GIS at Penn State in 2027.

Before GIS, I served in the U.S. Navy as a nuclear-trained Machinist's Mate (MMN) and Engineering Laboratory Technician (ELT) on submarines, responsible for reactor chemistry and radiological controls. That work taught me to keep precise records, follow strict procedures, and trust data only after checking it.

Projects

Where Is the Chokepoint? Southeast Asian trafficking networks

GEOINT capstone. A network analysis of 1,271 cross-border records from the CTDC synthetic trafficking dataset, using betweenness centrality, node-removal and cut-vertex tests, and community detection to ask which Southeast Asian country, if any, is the chokepoint in the regional sexual-exploitation network. Python (pandas, NetworkX, GeoPandas) and ArcGIS Pro.

Galapagos Marine Monitoring: system design

Class project for GEOG 583. A GIS system design proposal for routing illegal fishing and forced labor alerts in the Galapagos to the authority that can act: user analysis, a comparison of three software stacks, wireframes, system architecture, database design and a cost/benefit analysis built around ArcGIS Enterprise.
